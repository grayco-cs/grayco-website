Water damage? What to do in the first 24 hours

  1. Stop the waterShut off the main water valve (often near the street meter, in a garage or crawlspace) or the valve at the fixture. If water is near outlets or the panel, turn off power to that area at the breaker — only if you can reach it from a dry spot.
  2. Stay safeKeep people and pets out of rooms with sagging ceilings or standing water. Sewage or flood water from outside is contaminated — do not walk through it.
  3. Call GrayCo 24/7: (901) 585-0944Tell us what happened and where the water is. We give you an arrival time and what to do until we get there.
  4. Photograph everythingTake photos and video of the water, the source and every damaged item before anything is moved or thrown away. Keep the broken part (supply line, valve) if you can.
  5. Move what you canLift furniture onto blocks or foil, move rugs, electronics and papers to a dry room. Do not use a household vacuum on water.
  6. Report the claimNotify your insurance company and write down the claim number. Ask what your policy covers for emergency mitigation — it is usually expected that you prevent further damage.
  7. Start drying within 24–48 hoursWet drywall, insulation and flooring start growing problems quickly. Professional drying with moisture readings is what proves the structure is dry before it is closed back up.

Will my homeowners insurance cover water damage?

Sudden and accidental water damage — a burst pipe or failed supply line — is often covered, while long-term leaks and outside flood water may not be. Coverage depends on your own policy.
